# Open Contribution & Canonical Project Protection Policy

**Policy version:** 1.0  
**Publication date:** 10 August 2026  
**Grace period:** 30 calendar days from publication  
**Grace-period end:** 9 September 2026

## 1. Purpose

This repository is intended to remain usable for legitimate development, experimentation, forks, derivative projects, integrations and community contribution.

This policy is not intended to interrupt independent projects, rewrite their history, impose retrospective charges, or prevent good-faith contributors from continuing their work. Its purpose is to protect the integrity, security, operational resources, attributable lineage and canonical authority of this project while preserving an open contribution environment.

## 2. Core separation

**OPEN USE ≠ CANONICAL AUTHORITY ≠ EXECUTION AUTHORITY ≠ DEPLOYMENT AUTHORITY ≠ SPENDING AUTHORITY.**

Permission to view, fork, branch from, develop against, or otherwise use repository content under the applicable licence does not by itself grant authority to:

- modify a protected canonical branch;
- access protected secrets or credentials;
- use project-funded or restricted execution resources;
- deploy into a protected or production environment;
- publish an official project release or package;
- incur expenditure on behalf of the canonical project; or
- represent an independent project as an authorised canonical release.

## 3. Existing projects are not to be disturbed

Existing forks, contributor branches, derivative projects and integrations may continue subject to the applicable repository licence and law.

Introduction of this policy does not, by itself:

- delete or disable an existing independent project;
- rewrite its Git history;
- require its developer to abandon or migrate the project;
- create retrospective payment liability for ordinary historical usage; or
- convert independent work into canonical project property or authority.

Where a security or resource boundary must be changed, the preferred response is isolation or redirection of privileged access rather than destruction of contributor work.

## 4. Thirty-day transition and acceptance period

Existing contributors receive a 30-calendar-day transition period beginning on the publication date.

During the grace period, contributors may review the policy and decide whether they wish to continue only as independent users/contributors or participate in privileged canonical-project activities.

Acceptance is required for future privileged participation in protected canonical resources. Acceptance is not required merely to view public source or maintain an otherwise permitted independent fork.

Suggested transition notices:

- T0: policy publication and initial notice;
- T+15 days: courtesy reminder;
- T+25 days: final transition reminder;
- T+30 days: privileged canonical access requires an accepted current policy status.

Failure to accept by the end of the grace period does not destroy or disable an otherwise permitted independent project. It means only that protected canonical-project privileges are withheld until the applicable terms are accepted and authority is granted.

## 5. No automatic retrospective charges

This policy does not create an automatic claim for payment or reimbursement for historical GitHub Actions, runners, infrastructure or related usage.

Historical usage may be recorded for attribution, security review, operational analysis and architecture correction. Separate cases involving an express agreement, fraud, abuse, compromise or unlawful conduct are outside this non-retrospective assurance and must be assessed on their own facts.

## 6. Canonical protection boundary

The default branch and any branch, environment or release channel formally designated as canonical may be protected.

Independent branches and forks remain separate working areas. Promotion into the canonical project should occur through an attributable review path, ordinarily a pull request with applicable checks and authorised review.

Canonical protection should not be implemented by deleting unrelated contributor work.

## 7. Accountability and provenance

Accountability means traceability, not a presumption of wrongdoing.

Where technically available, an activity capable of affecting canonical resources should be attributable to evidence including:

- actor/account;
- repository or fork;
- branch/ref;
- commit SHA;
- pull request, where applicable;
- workflow and run/job identifier;
- runner/resource class;
- protected environment or resource affected;
- timestamp;
- result/status; and
- approving or merging authority, where applicable.

## 8. Protected resources

Repository visibility or contribution rights do not automatically expose protected resources.

The following should require separately granted authority where applicable:

- production and deployment secrets;
- cloud or infrastructure credentials;
- package-publishing credentials;
- protected environments;
- paid, restricted or self-hosted runners;
- production deployment controls;
- official release/publishing authority; and
- other resources capable of creating material cost or operational impact.

Contributor workflows should, where practicable, operate without production secrets or canonical deployment authority.

## 9. Pull requests as the canonical admission boundary

Independent development may occur in branches or forks. A request to incorporate work into a protected canonical branch should use the repository's approved review process.

Checks, review and merge authority are controls over canonical admission; they are not ownership claims over unrelated independent projects.

## 10. Attribution and representation

Contributor attribution and repository history should be preserved according to the applicable licence, platform capabilities and project rules.

Conversely, independent derivative work must not be represented as an official or authorised canonical project release merely because it originated from this repository.

## 11. Transition states

For governance and audit purposes, privileged participation may be represented by states such as:

- **LEGACY-PRE-POLICY** — activity identified before this policy;
- **GRACE** — contributor is within the transition period;
- **ACCEPTED** — current policy accepted for applicable privileged participation;
- **INDEPENDENT** — contributor continues independently without protected canonical privileges;
- **EXPIRED-GRACE** — grace period elapsed without acceptance; independent permissible work remains unaffected;
- **SUSPENDED** — specified privileged authority temporarily suspended for a recorded reason; and
- **REVOKED** — previously granted project authority formally withdrawn.

`EXPIRED-GRACE` must not be interpreted as automatic deletion or disabling of an independent project.

## 12. Security and emergency containment

Nothing in the grace period prevents immediate proportionate containment of an active security incident, compromised credential, malicious workflow, destructive operation or material threat to protected infrastructure.

Containment should be scoped to the threatened privilege, credential, workflow or resource wherever reasonably possible so unrelated contributor work is not unnecessarily affected.

## 13. Change management

Future policy amendments should be versioned and should identify their publication/effective date, transition requirements and supersession relationship.

Material changes affecting existing privileged participants should receive reasonable notice unless urgent security containment requires immediate action.

## 14. Governing principle

> **Protect the root without cutting the branches.**

The repository may remain a place where people build. Canonical authority, protected execution, deployment, expenditure and official representation remain separately governed and attributable.
